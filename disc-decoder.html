01 — THE CONCEPT

Two numbers, two different moments

Turn +1 → −5

What happens early — while the disc is still fast and spinning hard. A low (negative) number means it wants to roll over toward your anhyzer side. A number near +1 means it just refuses to.

Fade 0 → 5

What happens late — once the disc has slowed down and lost its gyroscopic fight against gravity. A high number means a hard hook back to the left at the very end. Zero means it just dies straight.

Why the scale is +1 to −5, not something clean like 0–10 Innova built this system by feel, not physics — pros rating discs against each other, not a measured unit like degrees. Turn and Fade aren't even the same kind of thing: Turn measures resistance, so most discs cluster near "doesn't turn much," which is why the positive side is short (it tops out at +1). Fade only ever has one direction to describe, so it just runs 0 up to "a lot." The lopsided ranges reflect what actually varies across real discs, not a formula. Don't look for hidden meaning in the exact numbers — just get a feel for where a disc sits relative to your other ones.

03 — WHAT THE NUMBERS ASSUME

The four numbers don't tell you

The stamped numbers describe one disc, fresh out of the box, thrown flat with enough arm speed to actually reach its rated Speed. Change any of those conditions and the real flight moves.

Arm speed vs. rated speed

The biggest one. If you can't generate the speed a disc is rated for, it never gets to Turn — it just fades early and flies more overstable than the numbers promise. Way more common than people think.

Wear / plastic

Every disc gets more understable as it beats in. Base plastics (DX, Pro) soften and shift faster than premium blends (Champion, Star), which hold their numbers longer.

Weight

A lighter copy of the same mold flies a touch more understable; max-weight tends to run more overstable. Two 173g and 150g discs of the same mold aren't identical flyers.

Nose angle

Nose-up on release kills glide and shortens everything. Nose-down adds speed and turn, and can turf a disc into the ground early. This is about your release, not the disc.

Release angle

Hyzer or anhyzer at release stacks on top of the disc's own Turn/Fade. Anhyzer exaggerates turn, hyzer exaggerates fade — same disc, different shot shape.

Wind

Headwind exaggerates turn on understable discs and can flip them hard. Tailwind kills lift and makes discs fade earlier — reach more overstable in a tailwind, more understable into the wind.

The flight glyph

A one-second sketch of the bow from the decoder above, ending in an open circle at the landing spot — the same convention Innova's own charts use. Faster to read mid-round than four digits.

Stability letter

U understable, S stable, O overstable — the Turn+Fade sum, so you don't do the math standing on the tee.

Go-to release arrow

One small arrow for the shot you actually throw this disc on — flat, hyzer, or anhyzer — so the disc reminds you of its job, not just its numbers.
